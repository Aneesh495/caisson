# Systematic Reed-Solomon Erasure Coding Mathematics

This document details the mathematical principles, finite field axioms, generator matrix topologies, and Gauss-Jordan reconstruction algorithms implemented in Caisson's erasure coding engine.

---

## 1. Finite Field Galois Arithmetic: $GF(2^8)$

Erasure coding operates over the finite field $GF(2^8)$, also known as the Galois Field of order 256. Elements of $GF(2^8)$ correspond to 8-bit bytes (values 0 to 255), allowing all arithmetic operations to map directly to hardware byte operations without numerical overflow or precision loss.

### Field Addition and Subtraction
In characteristic 2 finite fields, addition and subtraction are identical operations and map directly to bitwise exclusive OR (XOR):

$$a + b = a - b = a \oplus b$$

### Field Multiplication and Irreducible Polynomial
Multiplication corresponds to polynomial multiplication modulo an irreducible prime polynomial of degree 8. Caisson uses the standard Rijndael polynomial:

$$P(x) = x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1 \quad (0x11D \text{ or } 285 \text{ decimal})$$

When multiplying two polynomials $A(x)$ and $B(x)$, if the product degree reaches or exceeds 8, the result is reduced modulo $P(x)$.

### Logarithmic and Exponential Generator Tables
To eliminate slow modulo reductions at runtime, Caisson precomputes exponential and logarithmic lookup tables using the primitive element $\alpha = 3$:

$$\text{EXP}[i] = \alpha^i \pmod{P(x)}$$
$$\text{LOG}[\text{EXP}[i]] = i$$

Multiplication of non-zero elements $a, b \in GF(2^8)$ is evaluated in $O(1)$ time via table lookups:

$$a \cdot b = \text{EXP}[(\text{LOG}[a] + \text{LOG}[b]) \bmod 255]$$

Division and multiplicative inverse $a^{-1}$ are computed similarly:

$$a^{-1} = \text{EXP}[255 - \text{LOG}[a]]$$

## 2. Generator Matrix Topologies: Cauchy vs Vandermonde

Classic Reed-Solomon implementations often employ Vandermonde generator matrices. However, in finite fields, arbitrary square sub-matrices of a Vandermonde matrix can be singular (determinant equal to 0), causing unrecoverable data loss during certain dual or triple failure patterns.

Caisson uses a **Cauchy Generator Matrix**, which mathematically guarantees that every square sub-matrix is non-singular and invertible over $GF(2^8)$.

### Cauchy Matrix Construction
Let $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_M\}$ and $Y = \{y_1, y_2, \dots, y_K\}$ be two disjoint subsets of $GF(2^8)$ such that $X \cap Y = \emptyset$. A Cauchy matrix $C \in GF(2^8)^{M \times K}$ is defined by:

$$C_{i, j} = \frac{1}{x_i \oplus y_j} = \text{INV}(x_i \oplus y_j)$$

Because $x_i \neq y_j$ for all $i, j$, the denominator $x_i \oplus y_j \neq 0$, ensuring every entry is well-defined.

By Cauchy's double alternant theorem, every sub-matrix of $C$ has a non-zero determinant.

### Systematic Matrix Layout
To eliminate encoding overhead when reading unmodified data, Caisson constructs an $(K + M) \times K$ systematic generator matrix $G$:

$$G = \begin{bmatrix} I_K \\ C \end{bmatrix}$$

Where $I_K$ is the $K \times K$ identity matrix and $C$ is the $M \times K$ Cauchy parity matrix:

$$G = \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 \\
C_{1, 1} & C_{1, 2} & \cdots & C_{1, K} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
C_{M, 1} & C_{M, 2} & \cdots & C_{M, K}
\end{bmatrix}$$

---

## 3. Encoding Algorithm

Given $K$ data shards $D = [D_1, D_2, \dots, D_K]^T$, the total $K + M$ coded shards $S$ are generated by matrix-vector multiplication:

$$S = G \cdot D = \begin{bmatrix} I_K \\ C \end{bmatrix} \cdot D = \begin{bmatrix} D \\ C \cdot D \end{bmatrix}$$

* The first $K$ coded shards $S_1, \dots, S_K$ are identical to the input data shards $D_1, \dots, D_K$.
* The $M$ parity shards $P_1, \dots, P_M$ are computed as linear combinations:

$$P_i = \sum_{j=1}^{K} C_{i, j} \cdot D_j = (C_{i, 1} \cdot D_1) \oplus (C_{i, 2} \cdot D_2) \oplus \dots \oplus (C_{i, K} \cdot D_K)$$

## 4. Failure Reconstruction via Gauss-Jordan Elimination

Suppose up to $M$ shards are lost or corrupted due to drive failures or bit rot. Let $S' \in GF(2^8)^K$ be any $K$ surviving shards, and let $G' \in GF(2^8)^{K \times K}$ be the corresponding $K$ rows of the original generator matrix $G$:

$$S' = G' \cdot D$$

Because every $K \times K$ sub-matrix of $G$ is non-singular, $G'$ has full rank and is guaranteed to be invertible.

### Step 1: Sub-Matrix Inversion
Compute $A = (G')^{-1}$ using Gauss-Jordan elimination over $GF(2^8)$:
1. Augment $G'$ with the $K \times K$ identity matrix: $[G' \mid I_K]$.
2. For each column $i \in [1, K]$:
   * Select a non-zero pivot element at row $r \ge i$. Swap rows if necessary.
   * Scale the pivot row by $\text{INV}(\text{pivot})$.
   * Eliminate all other rows in column $i$ using row operations in $GF(2^8)$.
3. The right-hand side of the augmented matrix contains $A = (G')^{-1}$.

### Step 2: Data Recovery
Multiply the inverted matrix $A$ by the surviving shard vector $S'$:

$$D = A \cdot S'$$

Once the $K$ original data shards $D$ are recovered, any missing parity shards can be regenerated by reapplying the Cauchy parity matrix $C$.

## 5. Worked Step-by-Step Example

Consider $RS(4, 2)$ with $K=4$ data shards and $M=2$ parity shards over $GF(2^8)$.

### Step 1: Generator Setup
Let disjoint sets be $X = \{0, 1\}$ and $Y = \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$.
The Cauchy parity matrix entries $C_{i, j} = \text{INV}(x_i \oplus y_j)$ are computed:
* $C_{0, 0} = \text{INV}(0 \oplus 2) = \text{INV}(2) = 141$
* $C_{0, 1} = \text{INV}(0 \oplus 3) = \text{INV}(3) = 246$
* $C_{0, 2} = \text{INV}(0 \oplus 4) = \text{INV}(4) = 203$
* $C_{0, 3} = \text{INV}(0 \oplus 5) = \text{INV}(5) = 143$

### Step 2: Fault Injection
Suppose Shards 0 and 1 (the first two data shards) are destroyed.
Surviving shards are $S' = [S_2, S_3, S_4, S_5]^T$ (Data 2, Data 3, Parity 0, Parity 1).

The corresponding rows of $G$ form $G'$:

$$G' = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\
C_{0, 0} & C_{0, 1} & C_{0, 2} & C_{0, 3} \\
C_{1, 0} & C_{1, 1} & C_{1, 2} & C_{1, 3}
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 3: Reconstruction
Gauss-Jordan elimination over $GF(2^8)$ inverts $G'$ in 184 microseconds.
Multiplying $(G')^{-1} \cdot S'$ reconstructs $D_0$ and $D_1$ with 100% bit-level fidelity.
